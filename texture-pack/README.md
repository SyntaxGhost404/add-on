# Terra Lucida

A naturalistic, high-fidelity resource pack for **Minecraft: Bedrock Edition 1.26.52.3 (26.52)** that runs
on the **standard renderer**. It needs no Vibrant Visuals, no ray tracing, no shader loader and no
experimental toggle, and it is built for phones first.

Every texture, sky element, colour map, particle sheet and atmosphere setting is original and generated by
the code in `tools/`. Vanilla files were used only for file names, paths, sizes, frame counts and functional
UV/alpha layouts.

## What you get

* **1,277 original textures**: every natural block, building block, colour family, copper stage, crop,
  flower, workstation, redstone part, rail, liquid, fire and portal; plus sun, moon phases, clouds, rain and
  snow, block-breaking cracks, enchantment glint, End sky, colour maps and particle sheets.
* **Three resolutions in one pack**: Lite 32x, Balanced 64x and Ultra 128x. The game picks the right one
  for the device, and you can switch in the pack settings.
* **Tiling that doesn't show**: 31 of the most common surfaces come in two or three weighted variants
  that the game scatters by position. They include stone and its varieties, deepslate, tuff, cobblestone,
  bricks, stone bricks, dirt, grass, sand, gravel, snow, moss and obsidian, and the variants reach 58 block
  keys including slabs and walls. Variants of faces the game never rotates share their edge band, so any mix
  tiles seamlessly. Soils, sand, snow, moss and concrete powder also rotate at random.
* **Depth without shaders**: each material is modelled as a height field and lit, with cavity shading, cool
  sky-filled crevices and warm highlights. Rock and masonry get deeper smooth-lighting corners, and snow
  gets softer ones.
* **Atmosphere for 89 biomes**: per-climate sky colours, aerial-perspective fog that starts at 45-62% of
  render distance (it hides chunk edges without shortening your view), heavier haze in rain, and water
  colour, clarity and underwater visibility that range from murky swamp to clear tropical sea.
* **Smooth animation**: water, lava, fire, soul fire, portals, kelp, seagrass, sea lanterns, lanterns,
  campfires, sculk, furnaces and more loop seamlessly with vanilla's frame counts.

## Install

1. Download `dist/Terra_Lucida_v1.0.0.mcpack` and open it. On Windows, double-click it. On Android or iOS,
   open it with Minecraft from Files, your browser or a chat app. Minecraft imports it and says so.
2. Go to **Settings > Global Resources > My Packs**, select **Terra Lucida** and **Activate** it.
3. Optional: press the **gear** on the pack to choose a resolution:

| Option | Resolution | Device tier | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lite 32x - older phones | 32x | 0 (any device) | Low-memory phones and tablets |
| Balanced 64x - most phones | 64x | 2 and up | Most phones and tablets (default on mid-range devices) |
| Ultra 128x - high-end devices | 128x | 4 and up | Flagship phones, consoles and PCs |

Devices below a tier can't select that option, and the game pre-selects the highest one your device
allows.

**Recommended video settings:** Vibrant Visuals **off** (the pack targets the standard renderer), Fancy
Graphics **on** (see-through leaves), Smooth Lighting **on** (needed for the corner-shading tuning) and
Beautiful Skies **on**.

The pack works in any world and on servers that allow global resource packs. It changes no gameplay. Ores,
redstone states, crop ages and the 16 dye colours stay easy to tell apart, because textures are
calibrated to vanilla's average colour and brightness.

## Building from source

The build is deterministic and needs Python 3.10+, numpy and Pillow.

```sh
python3 tools/build.py --jobs 4                                  # build/terra_lucida/ and dist/*.mcpack (~4 min)
python3 tools/build.py --only grass --no-dist                    # re-render a subset while working
python3 tools/validate.py --samples /path/to/bedrock-samples     # checks (see below)
```

`validate.py` checks the built pack without the game:

* **Manifest:** format 3 layout, UUIDs and version strings, subpack folders and memory tiers, and language
  keys.
* **Textures:** every texture in every tier has the vanilla file type, the right size and frame count, and
  vanilla's alpha class (opaque stays opaque, cut-outs stay 0/255). Textures the game tints must stay grey.
  No stray files.
* **Definitions:**
  * terrain variations resolve in every tier;
  * `blocks.json` uses only real blocks and texture keys, and keeps every vanilla field;
  * fog and client-biome files keep vanilla's names and identifiers, so they override vanilla rather than
    add to it;
  * fog distances and colours are in range.
* **Budget:** the terrain atlas of each tier against the atlas size that tier targets.

With a checkout of [Mojang/bedrock-samples](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples), the manifest is
also checked against Mojang's packaging 3.0.0 schema, and client biomes against the biome schema for their
format version.

| Path | Contents |
|---|---|
| `tools/engine/` | Periodic noise, drawing primitives (strokes, fibres, fractures, grains), material finishing, tier export |
| `tools/recipes/` | One module per material family; `environment.py` holds sky, fog and biomes; `tuning.py` holds `blocks.json` |
| `tools/registry.py` | Maps every output to its vanilla path, variation keys and `blocks.json` overrides |
| `tools/data/` | Vanilla metadata: paths, sizes, frame counts, alpha classes, mean colours, UV silhouettes, key tables, fog and biome definitions |
| `tools/build.py`, `packaging_.py` | Build, manifest and `.mcpack` packaging |
| `tools/validate.py` | Validator |
| `tools/preview.py`, `icon.py` | Isometric diorama renderer, pack icon |
| `docs/DESIGN.md` | Design document: levers, art direction, techniques, tiers, coverage |

`build/` is generated and not tracked.

## Originality

All textures and atmosphere settings were made for this project by the procedural code in `tools/`. No
vanilla or third-party texture was traced, upscaled, recoloured or sampled, and the look doesn't imitate any
existing pack. The vanilla definition files in `tools/data/` and the vanilla fields that the generated
`blocks.json`, `fogs/` and `biomes/` carry over come from Mojang's published samples. The pack needs them to
override vanilla definitions in place.
